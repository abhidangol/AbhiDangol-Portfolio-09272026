Challenge
Living with kidney disease can be isolating and tiring. The app had to feel supportive and simple, never clinical or overwhelming.
What we did
  • Designed onboarding that asks who you are and what you need
  • Built a five-tab structure around community and daily care
  • Designed a forum where doctors and patients share posts
  • Added nutrition, exercise and meditation, open to contributions
  • Added a mood check-in and medical logs
  • Designed news, messages and actionable notifications

06

Designdecisions

Six choices that make a serious topic feel human.

Build
One calm green

A single olive green marks every action and active state. Everything else stays neutral, so nothing shouts.

Calm, not clinical
A friendly guide

An olive mascot welcomes people, delivers the confirmation email and celebrates the verified account.

A face to the app
Always a way to speak

A "Contribute" bar sits above feeds, so sharing a question, recipe, workout or story is one tap away.

Join, don't just read
Review before publishing

Recipes and exercises go "for review" with a clear confirmation, so shared advice can be checked.

Trust
Routine on Home

A week strip and today's appointment sit on Home and in Health, next to the numbers that matter that day.

Daily care
Alerts you can act on

Notifications carry their own answers, such as "Yes / Not yet" for medication, without opening another screen.

Fewer taps

Reflection
Lessons

What I learned

  • Warmth is a feature.For a serious topic, tone and a friendly guide do real work.
  • Make sharing easy.A community only exists if contributing takes one tap.
  • Speed leaves gaps.Auditing my own MVP showed where care and safety needed more thought.
Next

What I'd do next

  • Design the doctor side.Verification, answering tools and a clear badge.
  • Make it kidney-specific.Fluid, diet and appointments as first-class data.
  • Test with patients.Check type size, contrast and tone with real people.

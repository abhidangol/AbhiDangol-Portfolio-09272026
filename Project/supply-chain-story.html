Challenge

Distill a sprawling operations dataset into a single, calm screen — one that surfaces the shipments that need attention first, without turning the interface into a warehouse of reports and figures.

What we did
  • Redesigned the core tracking workspace around live shipment status
  • Built a risk-first triage model dispatchers actually trust
  • Designed the responsive system used across web, tablet and mobile
  • Ran usability sessions with dispatch teams across three release cycles
  • Shipped the rollout documentation and onboarding flows

Finaldecisions

The small rules that make a supply chain tool trustworthy.

Refine

Money, dates and status can't contradict themselves.

One rule for each recurring detail, applied on both surfaces.

Detail Before Decision
Currency $ on mobile, Rs on web Rs on both, grouped 5,20,000
Totals Line items and subtotal disagreed Subtotal + delivery + tax = total
Verification Title said phone, text said email The channel is named once
Password Two different rules 8+ characters, upper, number, symbol
Product ID "FKU" SKU
Empty KPI "Not Eligible" "No data yet" and a next step
Dates Three formats 24 Sep 2024
Order status Different names on web and mobile One set, same colours

One question per field

Retailer, category, product, variant. Each choice narrows the next.

Reflection
Lessons

What I learned

  • Design the connections first.Mapping how orders, stock and accounts move between roles made every later choice easier.
  • Same data, different density.The office needs breadth, the field needs speed.
  • Consistency is a feature.Money, dates and status are where trust is won or lost.
Next

What I'd do next

  • Test with real sales reps.On slow connections and in bright light.
  • Design the other two dashboards.Wholesaler and retailer, already supported by onboarding.
  • Handle offline orders.Shops don't always have signal.
